# The Brain and the brain: what inspired each region, how it is built, and where it maps

Written 4 Nov 2026 from the code, the defaults in `src/brain/config.py`, and the two research reports in `../reports/`. Generated by `docs/diagrams/brain_map.py`; edit that file, not this one.

**How to say it.** The Brain is brain-inspired: it models functions of a brain (attention, prediction error, sleep, habit) as engineering. Nothing here claims it is conscious or that it works like a brain. Every 'maps to' below is an analogy; sources marked *to verify* are not in the research notes and must be checked before the report quotes them.

## The path of one reading

Senses (perception and the Guardian's verdict) feed the forward models, which feed triage; triage opens an incident for the reflex tier at once and for the workspace, which ranks it; an ignited incident recruits packs and may ask the team; reflex and team both propose to the gate, the only way out; the Twin carries out the call; a closed incident becomes an episode in memory, and sleep compiles verified plans back into reflexes. On the canopy each of these is a region inside a glass brain, placed by its analogue: the senses at the back, the forward models in the cerebellum, triage deep in the middle, the workspace, packs and team at the front, memory low in the temporal lobe, the gate on the brain stem, the Twin outside.

## Region by region

### Senses: Sensory input and thalamic gating

*On the canopy:* back of the cortex.

**What inspired it.** The thalamic reticular nucleus works as a searchlight that gates what reaches cortex (Crick 1984; Halassa and Kastner 2017). Input is weighted by how reliable it is (Ernst and Banks 2002), and attention can be read as inferring each input's precision (Feldman and Friston 2010).

**How it is built.** `perception/` checks the contract version, the HMAC signature, the sequence number (replay) and the ranges, drops every field outside an allowlist (demographic fields never enter), and applies renames and units from config after the signature check. The Guardian then judges each reading (`POST /verdicts`, 2 s deadline, degraded mode if silent). Trust reaches attention as a damping factor: a trusted verdict counts in full from confidence 0.9 and in proportion below.

**Numbers.** Accepted contract versions; `verdict_deadline_ms` 2000; `trust_full_at` 0.9; stable rejection codes such as `signature_invalid`, `replay_or_out_of_order`.

**Agents.** No agent. Agents only ever see typed values that came through here.

**Architecture.** 1 Interop edge and 2 Senses and trust

**Where the analogy stops.** A thalamus does not check signatures. The analogy is the *gating by reliability*; the reliability judgement itself comes from another team's layer, the Guardian.

### Forward models: Cerebellar internal models; prediction error

*On the canopy:* cerebellum.

**What inspired it.** The cerebellum predicts the sensory result of an action and the error drives correction (Wolpert, Miall and Kawato 1998). Cortex answers a rare stimulus more than a common one (Ulanovsky, Las and Nelken 2003). The nervous system cancels the expected result of its own movement (Blakemore, Wolpert and Frith 1998; Crapse and Sommer 2008). Learning has a fast and a slow memory (Smith, Ghazizadeh and Shadmehr 2006) and a rate that follows volatility (Nassar et al. 2010; Behrens et al. 2007).

**How it is built.** `mind/forward.py`: per sensor and field, a running mean and variance. A reading many spreads from the prediction is a *surprise*. The first of a run is *doubted* and the reflex that would act on it is held (`brain.action.blocked`, check `surprise`); the next in the same direction confirms; three in a row make a new normal. A surprising reading never moves the baseline, so a spoofed spike cannot teach itself as normal. Only trusted readings teach; a retracted reading is forgotten and the baseline rebuilt; a previous normal is remembered and can be recovered. `mind/effects.py` is the corollary discharge: after an action the gate let through, the declared effect is expected, not doubted. `mind/neighbours.py` follows the difference between two sensors that should move together.

**Numbers.** `alpha` 0.2, `warmup` 5, `z_threshold` 4.0 (the Academy proposed 3.75, not enabled), `adopt_after` 3, a spread floor per field and 2% of the value, `history` 200. Off: `calibration: quantile`, `effects` and `coupled` (both wait for the Twin team to say what its actuators do and which sensors move together).

**Agents.** No agent. A doubted reading stops reflexes and lowers nothing an agent sees except the surprise feature.

**Architecture.** 2 Senses and trust. Plan 2 adds a Kalman tracker (level and rate) here.

**Where the analogy stops.** Surprise is a z-score, not Bayesian surprise (Itti and Baldi 2009). The learning rate is fixed on purpose: an adaptive one would open a route for an attacker to teach the Brain a spike. The 'fast and slow memory' is only the remembered previous normal.

### Triage: Amygdala 'low road': fast, coarse threat appraisal

*On the canopy:* deep in the middle.

**What inspired it.** A fast subcortical route reacts to threat before slower cortical analysis finishes (LeDoux 2000); the salience network flags what matters (Menon and Uddin 2010).

**How it is built.** `triage/` and the packs' detectors open incidents deterministically: crime from CCTV anomaly types at confidence 0.5 or more, flood from a water level of 1.0 m (cleared at 0.6 m), overload at 95% of capacity, smog at AQI 150, and fire, accident and jam from their detectors. Evidence joins an open incident within 300 s (crime) or 600 s (others).

**Numbers.** Seven incident types; `min_confidence` 0.5; merge windows 300 s and 600 s.

**Agents.** No agent. Triage decides what exists to think about; it never calls a model.

**Architecture.** 2 Senses and trust into 3 Understanding

**Where the analogy stops.** No valence or learning: these are rules written in pack files. The parallel is speed and coarseness, not mechanism.

### Workspace: Global neuronal workspace; salience network; limited capacity

*On the canopy:* front, top.

**What inspired it.** A content that crosses a threshold 'ignites' and is broadcast to many specialised processors (Mashour, Roelfsema, Changeux and Dehaene 2020). Working memory holds about four chunks (Cowan 2001; Luck and Vogel 1997) or a shared resource (Ma, Husain and Bays 2014). Activity is scarce for energy reasons (Attwell and Laughlin 2001). Urgency grows with waiting (Cisek, Puskas and El-Murr 2009).

**How it is built.** `mind/salience.py` scores each incident: a weighted mean of severity, trust, corroboration, novelty and clustering, multiplied by the share of trusted evidence and by freshness, plus a surprise boost damped the same way, so an incident on untrusted readings scores zero. `mind/workspace.py` holds two slots: an incident ignites at 0.5, releases under 0.3, and a newcomer must beat the weakest by 0.1 (hysteresis). Only incident types an agent team can act on compete for a slot; a flood or a jam is lit and broadcast without one. Ignition, release and deferral are timeline events.

**Numbers.** Weights severity 3, trust 3, corroboration 2, novelty 1, clustering 1; freshness half-life 120 s; surprise boost 0.25; 2 slots; `ignite_at` 0.5, `release_below` 0.3, `margin` 0.1. Off: `aging_per_s` (urgency), cap 0.3.

**Agents.** The gatekeeper to the agents: only a lit incident may ask the team to deliberate. Two slots because the free model plan allows 8,000 tokens a minute.

**Architecture.** Between 2 and 4. In plan 2 an ignition also starts the planning supervisor.

**Where the analogy stops.** Two slots, not four: the limit is the model budget, not biology. Ignition is a threshold with hysteresis, not attractor dynamics (Wang 2002), which the research advised against coding literally. Divisive normalisation (Reynolds and Heeger 2009) was tried for load shedding and cut: it moved no number.

### Packs: Specialised modules competing; degeneracy

*On the canopy:* front.

**What inspired it.** Many different parts can produce the same output, and that is a feature of complex systems (Edelman and Gally 2001). The workspace recruits specialised processors to a broadcast content (Mashour et al. 2020).

**How it is built.** Six department packs as data (`departments/manifests/`: Police, Fire, Ambulance and medical, Traffic control, Disaster response, Utilities), each with what it subscribes to, detectors, actuators, needs and severity. On ignition each pack bids (`mind/bids.py`): its fit for the incident type, scaled by the share of the actuators it needs that the Twin really offers and by free units. A pack that bids 0.5 or more joins. `brain.pack.uncovered` says when nobody can answer. A new pack is a file in `brain.packs_dir`.

**Numbers.** `recruit_at` 0.5; bids never stop a reflex, the gate checks anyway.

**Agents.** A pack names its agent team and its tools in the manifest (Police has its team of three; the other five packs each have a desk of two).

**Architecture.** 4 Decision. Phase 2 adaptability: a partner with a new kind of sensor or service gets a pack file, not code.

**Where the analogy stops.** Bids are computed, not learned.

### Reflex: Reflex arcs; habits

*On the canopy:* centre.

**What inspired it.** Fixed fast responses run without deliberation; a habitual system competes with a slower goal-directed one, and the choice between them is a trade-off of time against accuracy (Keramati, Dezfouli and Piray 2011).

**How it is built.** `graph/incident_graph.py`: one LangGraph thread per incident (`enrich, reflex, gate, actuate`), no model on the path; each new piece of evidence re-enters the same checkpointed thread. Thirteen rules over four departments propose actions; suspect scoring is a logistic score of behaviour only. A plan learned in sleep is applied here instead of calling the team (`brain.plan.compiled`): the habit.

**Numbers.** Suspect score: bias -3, co-presence 2, flight 2.5, repeat 1.5, direct 4; person of interest at 0.5, suspect at 0.8; flight settles after 10 s. First action 35 to 58 ms after an incident opens (measured, in process).

**Agents.** Not an agent, but it asks the team to deliberate in the background and carries on.

**Architecture.** 4 Decision, the fast path. Plan 2 puts the OR-Tools station chooser inside it.

**Where the analogy stops.** Rules are authored, not learned by repetition, except the plans sleep promotes, which must pass six checks.

### Team: Prefrontal goal maintenance; anterior cingulate conflict monitoring

*On the canopy:* front, lower.

**What inspired it.** Prefrontal cortex holds goals and rules that bias processing elsewhere (Miller and Cohen 2001). The anterior cingulate monitors conflict and signals when control is needed (Botvinick et al. 2001), weighed by its expected value (Shenhav, Botvinick and Cohen 2013). Confidence can be a second-order inference (Fleming and Daw 2017).

**How it is built.** `departments/team.py` and `builder.py`: Analyst (a case file: what the evidence supports and what could be wrong), Tactician (options, every zone from a graph algorithm), Chief (one option, justified from the playbook), then `verify_case` and `verify_plan` check the answer against the tool log and the playbook, with one revision round, before the gate. The roles mirror Planning, Operations and Command of the Incident Command System. Each role is a LangChain agent with read-only tools on Groq, or a scripted stand-in; a role whose model fails falls back to the stand-in.

**Numbers.** Analyst and Chief `gpt-oss-120b`, Tactician `gpt-oss-20b`, temperature 0; scripted by default, `--llm` opts in. Measured: on the earlier `b2` (the crime story now in `d3`), 5 of 5 real-model trials passed, 4 placed a plan before the arrest, plan agreement 0.75. The same prompt gave 5 different answers in 5 calls.

**Agents.** **This is where the agents live.** Every other region is code.

**Architecture.** 4 Decision, the slow path. Plan 2's supervisor runs the solver and the team in parallel and keeps the better feasible plan; plan 0 records every model call so a run can be replayed.

**Where the analogy stops.** The three roles are an organisational analogy (incident command), not three brain areas. The 'cingulate' here is deterministic code. There is no second-order confidence yet.

### Gate: Basal ganglia: default inhibition, release of the winner, a brake under conflict

*On the canopy:* on the brain stem (the way out).

**What inspired it.** The basal ganglia hold competing actions inhibited and select one by releasing it (Redgrave, Prescott and Gurney 1999; Humphries and Gurney 2021). The subthalamic nucleus raises the threshold when responses conflict, holding a premature response (Frank 2006).

**How it is built.** `gate/` is the only code that calls an actuator. Every proposal passes, in order: schema, capability (the Twin lists the tool), the department's allowlist, the incident is open, a trusted and unexpired verdict covers the evidence, enough independent trusted sources for the impact, human approval for high impact, no repeat (idempotency), cooling gap, the Guardian has not quarantined the actuator, and the action budget. Then it signs the call. A held action is reported with its reason as prominently as a sent one.

**Numbers.** Impact: valve and grid high (2 sources and a person), dispatch medium (2 sources), alert, signal and recall low (1 source); 6 actions per incident, 20 a minute; witnesses counted by sensor. One call site for `call_actuator` in the whole code.

**Agents.** Agents cannot reach it except through a proposal; their tools are read-only.

**Architecture.** 5 Safety. Plan 1 turns the human-approval refusal into a signed approval.

**Where the analogy stops.** A learned gate (O'Reilly and Frank 2006) was rejected on purpose: it would be opaque. The canopy draws it on the brain stem because it is the exit; the basal ganglia are deep forebrain nuclei, not the stem.

### Memory: Hippocampal index of episodes; semantic memory

*On the canopy:* low in the temporal lobe.

**What inspired it.** The hippocampus stores an index of an episode's cortical pattern, so a partial cue can bring it back (Teyler and Rudy 2007). An episodic log replayed in quiet time is the basis of consolidation (Mattar and Daw 2018).

**How it is built.** Episodes (`mind/episodes.py`): each closed incident's readings as typed values, people renamed `p-1`, `p-2`, the verdicts and each tier's decision; no identity, no unit id, and never whether a dispatch worked. The knowledge base: 68 items (31 procedures, 13 rules, 6 actuators, 18 interface chunks), hybrid search filtered before the cut. The city graph in memory, mirrored to Neo4j. Similar past stories without outcome or identity.

**Numbers.** Retrieval recall@3 0.956 with the real embedder on 45 questions; GraphRAG a measured null result, off. Episodes kept only when `brain.episodes_dir` is set.

**Agents.** Agents read it through tools (`kb.search`, stories when switched on); they never write to it.

**Architecture.** 3 Understanding and the memory stores. Plan 3 adds Qdrant for vectors and stories.

**Where the analogy stops.** A brain reconstructs memories and edits them on recall (Nader, Schafe and LeDoux 2000). The Brain's record must not change: the log is hash-chained, and episodes are typed copies.

### Sleep: Replay and consolidation; tag and capture; useful forgetting

*On the canopy:* back, low.

**What inspired it.** Experience is replayed in sleep, sometimes in reverse (Foster and Wilson 2006), with priority to what is useful (Mattar and Daw 2018). A weak trace is tagged and captured only if later confirmed (Frey and Morris 1997). Sleep renormalises what waking strengthened (Tononi and Cirelli 2014). Forgetting removes what is out of date (Richards and Frankland 2017). Retrieval makes a memory editable again (Nader, Schafe and LeDoux 2000).

**How it is built.** `brain sleep` (`mind/sleep.py`): mine episodes for plans the team made the same way at least twice, rehearse each candidate in simulation against the real team, and promote it as a reflex only through six checks: provenance (every reading trusted, none retracted), safety (only contain, respond and alert, inside the Police allowlist), a size cap, rehearsal (same decisions, fewer team runs), variants (neighbouring stories stay with the team), and the whole Test Book identical. `mind/lifecycle.py`: a lesson is confirmed only when the team plans its pattern the same way again, retired when it plans it differently, and may fade after sleeps with no recurrence.

**Numbers.** `min_support` 2, at most 10 learned rules, `retire_on_contradiction` on, `fade_after_sleeps` 0 (off). Measured on the earlier `b2` (now `d3`): team runs 1 to 0, time to the last action 123.6 ms to 79.7 ms, identical decisions.

**Agents.** Sleep learns *from* the agents: what it compiles is a cache of a verified team plan.

**Architecture.** 6 Learning. Plan 3 adds `brain improve`, one audited cycle.

**Where the analogy stops.** No reward signal. A brain learns from outcomes (reward prediction error); the Brain is forbidden to, because a learner that reads outcomes can be taught by an attacker who controls the sensors.

### Twin: The body and the world

*On the canopy:* outside the brain, beside the city.

**What inspired it.** Sensors and effectors: the periphery the brain senses through and acts on.

**How it is built.** Another team's layer, stood in by `mocks/twin/` over MCP: readings by `list_readings`, actions by tools named after them.

**Numbers.** The mock pursuit lasts 15 s, so a team can answer within it.

**Agents.** None.

**Architecture.** Outside the Brain, reached through `TwinPort`.

**Where the analogy stops.** The Brain has never talked to the real Twin yet.

## What the Brain does not take from biology, and why

| Not taken | Why |
| --- | --- |
| A bus of global neuromodulator values (dopamine, acetylcholine as broadcast numbers) | An alert-level scalar was cut: its inputs already shape salience |
| A learned gate in front of the actuators | Opaque; breaks 'code disposes' |
| Attractor-network ignition coded literally | The margin already gives the behaviour: amplify the difference, commit, keep the commitment |
| Learning from the outcome of a dispatch | An attacker who controls a sensor could teach it |
| Any claim of consciousness | The Brain models functions of a brain: attention, prediction error, sleep, habit |
